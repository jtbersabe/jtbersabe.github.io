About Me

I am a PhD student in the Department of Geography at Kyungpook National University, South Korea, supervised by Prof. Byong-Woon Jun. My research sits at the intersection of applied urban geography, spatial analytics, and natural disaster studies.

I am broadly interested in how cities are exposed to environmental hazards — particularly urban flooding — and how socially vulnerable populations bear disproportionate risks. My work integrates GIS, remote sensing, and machine learning to map flood susceptibility and assess environmental justice in urban contexts.

My master's thesis, "Environmental Justice Assessment of Urban Flood Risk in Seoul Using Machine Learning and Intelligent Dasymetric Mapping," combined flood susceptibility modelling with fine-grained population distribution data to uncover spatial inequities in flood exposure across Seoul.

I am passionate about:

  • Developing machine learning pipelines for urban flood susceptibility mapping
  • Integrating multi-source gridded population datasets for disaster exposure assessment
  • Applying dasymetric mapping and spatial statistics to environmental justice questions
  • Translating geospatial research into actionable insights for urban planners and policymakers
